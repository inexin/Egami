Bitmap tracing

Bitmap tracing turns a raster into filled vector shapes: a scanned signature, line art or a logo comes back as Path objects with a color each, ready to draw at any size, record as a Picture, or save as SVG. It is part of Egami.Drawing, in the namespace Egami.Drawing.Tracing; Egami.Svg adds the SVG output.

Four modes trace filled shapes: black and white, grayscale, color and logo. Centre-line and pixel-art modes come in later 1.x versions, as new TraceMode values; traced output may also improve between minor versions.

Tracing a raster

raster.Trace(options) traces the raster's first frame and returns a TraceResult: its Shapes, back to front, and the traced image's Width and Height.

// Trace a scan into shapes: each ink region becomes a black path of curves and corners, back to front.
TraceResult result = scan.Trace(TraceOptions.BlackAndWhite);

foreach (TracedShape shape in result.Shapes)
{
    Console.WriteLine($"{shape.Color} {shape.Path.Bounds}");
}

// The shapes are in the scan's pixels; scale them like any path.
Path ink = result.ToPath(Rgba8.Black).Transform(Matrix3x2.CreateScale(0.5f));
  • Each TracedShape has a Path, a Color (Rgba8), a Kind (TracedShapeKind.Fill or Stroke) and, for a stroke, a StrokeWidth. The black-and-white, grayscale, color and logo modes give filled shapes only.
  • Coordinates are the raster's pixels, y down, with pixel (0, 0) covering the square from (0, 0) to (1, 1), as on a canvas.
  • In black and white, one shape is one ink region: its outer edge and its holes. A region inside another region's hole comes after it. Palette lists the shapes' colors, each once, back to front.
  • Trace (in RasterTraceExtensions) works on any pixel type; Trace<TPixel> is the same for a typed raster. TraceAsync traces on a worker thread and takes a CancellationToken, checked between rows and between outlines.
  • The raster's EgamiContext (or one you pass) limits the size: a raster larger than SafetyLimits.MaxPixels throws LimitExceededException.
// Trace on a worker thread; the token is checked between rows and between outlines.
TraceResult result = await scan.TraceAsync(TraceOptions.BlackAndWhite, cancellationToken: cancellationToken);

Options

TraceOptions is a record: start from a preset and change what you need with with. Each setting is checked when it is set.

// Black and white with every setting spelled out.
TraceResult ink = scan.Trace(new TraceOptions
{
    Mode = TraceMode.BlackAndWhite,
    Threshold = null,      // null: Otsu's method, as Raster.FindThreshold; or a level from 0 (black) to 1 (white)
    PathFidelity = 0.5f,   // 0: fewest curves; 1: curves that follow the pixels most closely
    Corners = 0.75f,       // 0: round every corner; 1: keep every corner (a polygon)
    Noise = 4,             // regions under 4 pixels merge into the regions around them
    IgnoreWhite = true,    // the ink alone, no white background shape
});

// Or start from a preset and change one thing.
TraceResult polygon = scan.Trace(TraceOptions.BlackAndWhite with { Corners = 1 });
Setting Default What it does
Mode BlackAndWhite How pixels become shapes: BlackAndWhite, Grayscale, Color or Logo.
Threshold null Black and white: the level from 0 (black) to 1 (white) that splits ink from paper. A pixel is ink when its intensity, over white by its alpha, is at most this. null finds it with Otsu's method, as Raster.FindThreshold does.
Colors 8 Grayscale, color and logo: the most colors (gray levels) the image is reduced to, 2 to 256. An image with fewer flat colors gets fewer.
Palette null Grayscale, color and logo: fixed colors to reduce the image to instead; the shapes take them exactly.
Layering Stacked Grayscale, color and logo: how the layers fit together (see below).
Smoothing 0 Grayscale and color: edge-keeping smoothing before the colors are found, from 0 to 1, for photos. Not used in logo.
PathFidelity 0.5 How closely the curves follow the pixel edges: 0 gives the fewest curves, 1 the closest fit. 0.5 is a tolerance of half a pixel.
Corners 0.25 How readily corners stay sharp: 0 rounds every corner, 1 keeps every corner (the outline is a polygon). 0.25 keeps any turn of more than 60 degrees.
SnapToLines true Straight runs of an outline become lines.
SnapToArcs true Runs on a circle become circular arcs, and round outlines circles.
Noise 2 The smallest region kept, in pixels. Smaller regions merge into the regions around them, smallest first (in grayscale and color, into the neighbour they share the most edge with), so a speck inside a small hole goes before the hole. 0 or 1 keeps everything.
IgnoreWhite false Leaves out white: in black and white the white shape that otherwise comes first, under the ink, the size of the image; in grayscale, color and logo the layer of each color whose red, green and blue are all at least 240, whose areas stay empty (in logo, also the transparency the art encloses).

TraceOptions.Default is the defaults. TraceOptions.BlackAndWhite is the defaults with IgnoreWhite: the ink alone.

Grayscale and color

TraceMode.Color finds the image's own flat colors, at most Colors of them, and traces each as a layer of shapes; TraceMode.Grayscale does the same with gray levels. Pixels along antialiased or blurred edges are blends of the colors on either side, so they are left out when the colors are found, and each takes the color of the side it is nearer.

// A flat-colored logo: up to 8 of its own colors, one layer each, largest first, each layer covering those above it.
TraceResult stacked = logo.Trace(new TraceOptions { Mode = TraceMode.Color, Colors = 8 });
IReadOnlyList<Rgba8> colors = stacked.Palette;     // the colors found, back to front

// Abutting layers: each color only its own area, neighbours sharing one edge, and white left out.
TraceResult pieces = logo.Trace(new TraceOptions
{
    Mode = TraceMode.Color,
    Layering = TraceLayering.Abutting,
    IgnoreWhite = true,
    SnapToLines = true,    // straight runs become lines
    SnapToArcs = true,     // runs on a circle become arcs, round outlines circles
});

// Grayscale to fixed levels, smoothed first as for a photo.
TraceResult poster = logo.Trace(new TraceOptions
{
    Mode = TraceMode.Grayscale,
    Palette = [new Rgba8(0, 0, 0, 255), new Rgba8(128, 128, 128, 255), new Rgba8(255, 255, 255, 255)],
    Smoothing = 0.5f,
});
  • TraceLayering.Stacked (the default) puts the color with the largest area first. Each layer covers its own pixels and those of the layers above it that it touches, so the layers above lie on it and no gap shows between colors when they are drawn antialiased. One shape is one connected part of a layer.
  • TraceLayering.Abutting gives each color only its own pixels, one shape per region. Where regions meet, the boundary is cut at the points where three meet, each piece is fitted once, and both neighbours use it, so they share their edge exactly and never overlap. This suits editing the shapes one by one.
  • Smoothing runs a bilateral filter, one to three passes, before the colors are found: texture and noise flatten while edges between clearly different colors stay put.

Logo

TraceMode.Logo is for logos, icons and other flat-color art with antialiased edges, where a few pixels decide whether a curve looks round. It finds the flat colors as color mode does, then splits every edge pixel into the two colors it blends and places the edge from a smooth field of how much of each pixel each color covers. The curves come back rounder than in color mode on small art: a disc of radius 6 keeps its radius to about 0.05 pixels.

// Flat-color art with antialiased edges: edges placed from smooth coverage fields, for rounder curves on small art.
TraceResult traced = logo.Trace(new TraceOptions { Mode = TraceMode.Logo, Colors = 8 });

// On a transparent icon the outside is left out; the hole the ring encloses is traced white unless IgnoreWhite.
TraceResult donut = icon.Trace(new TraceOptions { Mode = TraceMode.Logo, Layering = TraceLayering.Abutting });

// A photo or a gradient is not flat color, so logo mode refuses it: trace it with TraceMode.Color instead.
  • Transparency around the art is left out: no shape covers it. Transparency the art encloses, such as the inside of a letter O on a transparent background, is traced as white, which IgnoreWhite leaves out like any white.
  • Colors, Palette, Layering, Noise and the fit settings work as in color mode. A palette maps each of the image's own colors to the nearest fixed color; with transparency it takes at most 254 colors.
  • An image that is not flat color is refused with an ArgumentException: when more than a tenth of its pixels are neither one of its colors nor a blend of two, it is a photo or a gradient (trace it with TraceMode.Color), or it has more flat colors than Colors allows. JPEG noise on a flat logo is fine.
  • Small images are sampled finer, up to 4 × 4 samples a pixel, so a 100-pixel icon has as smooth an outline as a large one.

Shapes and paths

// TraceOptions.Default also gives a white shape for the paper, under the ink.
TraceResult result = scan.Trace();
TracedShape paper = result.Shapes[0];          // Color white, Kind Fill, the whole image
Path ink = result.ToPath(Rgba8.Black);          // every black shape as one path

// Outer edges run clockwise and holes counter-clockwise, so either fill rule fills the same area.
bool same = ink.Contains(new Vector2(40, 40)) == ink.WithFillRule(FillRule.EvenOdd).Contains(new Vector2(40, 40));
  • ToPath(color) joins the filled shapes of one color into one path, with the non-zero fill rule, or returns Path.Empty.
  • Outer edges run clockwise on screen and holes counter-clockwise, so NonZero and EvenOdd fill a traced path the same.
  • The paths are ordinary Path objects: transform them, combine them with PathOps, measure them, or write them as SVG path data with ToSvg.

Drawing and recording

Draw(canvas) draws the shapes in the canvas's current transform and clip, so a scale draws them larger with no stair steps. ToPicture() records them as a Picture the size of the traced image, to replay on any canvas, render with ToRaster or save as a drawing file.

TraceResult result = scan.Trace(TraceOptions.BlackAndWhite);

// Draw the shapes on any canvas, here four times as large.
using Raster<Rgba8> large = Raster.Create(scan.Width * 4, scan.Height * 4, Rgba8.White);
using (RasterCanvas<Rgba8> canvas = large.CreateCanvas())
{
    canvas.Scale(4, 4);
    result.Draw(canvas);
}

// Or record them as a picture to replay later, render, or save as a drawing file.
Picture picture = result.ToPicture();
using Raster<Rgba8> sharp = picture.ToRaster<Rgba8>(scale: 8);

SVG

Egami.Svg writes a trace through SvgCanvas (SvgTraceExtensions): ToSvg() returns the markup and SaveSvg writes a file or a stream, one path element per shape, back to front, in an SVG the size of the traced image. SvgWriteOptions sets the number precision.

// Egami.Svg writes a trace as SVG through SvgCanvas: one path element per shape.
TraceResult result = scan.Trace(TraceOptions.BlackAndWhite);
string markup = result.ToSvg(new SvgWriteOptions { Precision = 2 });
result.SaveSvg(file);

How tracing works

Each mode follows the same steps, written from the papers in the references:

  1. Labels. Black and white splits ink from paper at the threshold; ink pixels that touch only at a corner belong together (8-connected) and paper is 4-connected. Grayscale and color give each pixel its nearest palette color; regions are 4-connected. Regions under Noise pixels merge into their neighbours.
  2. Boundaries. The pixel edges between regions form closed loops, walked with the region on the right, after P. Selinger's "Potrace: a polygon-based tracing algorithm" (2003).
  3. Edge points. Each pixel edge gives a point, moved across the edge by how much the pixels on either side blend the two colors, so an antialiased edge lands where it was drawn. A hard staircase gives one point per step, on the line it draws, and a pixel corner between two sides stays.
  4. Corners. A point where the outline turns by more than the Corners angle, over a few points and over twice as many, is a corner; it moves to where lines fitted to the points on either side meet.
  5. Curves. Between corners, straight runs become lines and runs on a circle arcs; the rest is fitted with cubic curves by least squares, after P. J. Schneider, "An algorithm for automatically fitting digitized curves" (1990), split at the worst point until every point is within the PathFidelity tolerance.

A traced circle keeps its size to a few hundredths of a pixel when its edge is antialiased: a disc of radius 30 comes back with a radius of 29.95.

Logo mode replaces steps 1 and 3:

  1. Coverage. Each pixel is split into up to three shares: its flat color, or a blend of two colors (its own and a neighbour's), and, under alpha, transparency. Fringe colors that only blend others, along edges or from JPEG, leave the palette first.
  2. Fields. Each color's shares become a signed distance to its edge, after S. Gustavson and R. Strand, "Anti-aliased Euclidean distance transform" (2011), which a cubic B-spline interpolates between pixel centres, prefiltered as in M. Unser, A. Aldroubi and M. Eden, "B-spline signal processing" (1993) and P. Thévenaz, T. Blu and M. Unser, "Interpolation revisited" (2000).
  3. Iso-lines. The fields are sampled on a finer grid and each sample takes the color whose field leads there; the edge between two samples sits where the two fields cross, as in W. Lorensen and H. Cline's marching cubes (1987) in two dimensions. The rest is the color-mode walk and fit, scaled back to the image's pixels.
