Namespace Egami.Video.Codecs

Classes

Av1Codec

AV1, as WebM, Matroska, MP4, IVF and raw OBU files carry it, decoded by Egami's AV1 decoder in the Main, High and Professional profiles: 8, 10 and 12 bit, 4:0:0, 4:2:0, 4:2:2 and 4:4:4, film grain, scaled references and scalable streams, every frame equal to libaom's sample for sample. A packet is a temporal unit. Frames come out at their own size (a stream may change size at a key frame) as 8-bit planes, or 16-bit planes holding 10 or 12 bits; a monochrome stream gives 4:0:0 planes. Hidden frames give no frame; a frame shown later with show_existing_frame comes out then. Encoding goes through CreateEncoder(VideoEncoderOptions, MediaContext).

Av1EncoderOptions

The AV1 settings beyond VideoEncoderOptions, for experts: quantizer limits, hidden alternate reference frames and their pyramid, golden frame spacing, the temporal filter, adaptive quantization, screen content tools, error resilience and the bit depth. Every one has a default that suits most uses.

Av1Packets

Helpers for AV1 packets, which hold a temporal unit of low-overhead OBUs (AV1 specification section 5, each OBU with its size field): split a unit into its frames and merge them back, and build the av1C configuration record MP4 and Matroska store for a track.

CodecHeaderReader

Lists the header fields of a track's packets read in order, keeping what a codec's later headers depend on. For AV1 it decodes each temporal unit with Egami's Av1StreamDecoder, so each frame header is listed whole, as the decoder read it (LastHeaders, under the specification's syntax element and variable names), in place of the start Describe(TrackInfo, EncodedPacket) gives. Other codecs give what Describe(TrackInfo, EncodedPacket) gives.

CodecHeaders

Lists what a compressed frame's header says, field by field under the codec specification's names, for tools such as egami-video info --headers and for checking an encoder's output. VP8 (RFC 6386 sections 9 and 19), VP9 (VP9 bitstream specification sections 6.2 and 6.3), AV1 (AV1 specification section 5) and FFV1 (RFC 9043 section 4) are described; later codecs add theirs to the same call. An AV1 frame header depends on the reference frames, so CodecHeaderReader reads a track's packets in order to list them whole.

EncodeFrameOptions

What one Send(VideoFrame, EncodeFrameOptions) call asks for beyond the frame.

Ffv1Codec

FFV1, the lossless video codec archives use (RFC 9043), as Matroska and AVI carry it: versions 0, 1 and 3, Golomb-Rice and the range coder with the default or a custom state table, YCbCr 4:2:0, 4:2:2, 4:4:4, 4:1:1, 4:1:0 and gray with or without alpha, and RGB with or without alpha, at 8 to 16 bits, every frame equal to FFmpeg's sample for sample. Frames come out as their own planes: 8-bit planes, or 16-bit planes holding 10, 12 or 16 bits, where depths 9, 11, 13, 14 and 15 sit in the next of those with their samples shifted up. RGB comes out as G, B and R planes tagged Identity. Version 2, which was experimental, and 4:4:0 streams are not decoded.

Ffv1VerifyError

One problem Verify(string, MediaContext?, CancellationToken) found.

Ffv1VerifyResult

What Verify(string, MediaContext?, CancellationToken) found: how much it checked and every CRC that did not match. A version 3 stream with slice CRCs is checked whole; versions 0 and 1, and version 3 written without slice CRCs, have only what can be checked without decoding (the configuration record and the slice layout).

FirstPassStats

What the first pass of a two-pass encode learned about each frame: how hard it is to code. Pass it as FirstPassStats with Bitrate, and the second pass spreads the bits over the whole clip by it instead of guessing from the frames so far. Save(Stream) keeps it in a file, so the two passes can run apart, as FFmpeg's pass log does.

MotionJpegCodec

Motion JPEG: every frame a complete JPEG image, decoded and encoded by Egami's JPEG codec. Decoding fixes frames without a DHT segment, weaves interlaced field pairs, ignores junk around the image and decodes several frames at once. Frames come out as the JPEG's own YUV planes, BT.601 full range unless the container says otherwise.

RawVideoCodec

Uncompressed video (Uncompressed): I420, YV12, Y42B, 444P, Y800, NV12, NV21, YUY2, UYVY and v210 by FourCC (FourCC), plain planar YUV at 8 to 12 bits with optional alpha as in YUV4MPEG2, and AVI's bottom-up 24-bit BI_RGB (as VfwFourCC), whose frames come out as G, B and R planes (Identity). Decoding and encoding copy samples, so they are lossless.

VideoCodec

A video codec: which tracks it decodes and how to create its decoder and encoder. Register a new one with WithCodec(VideoCodec).

VideoCodecs

Vp8Codec

VP8 (RFC 6386), as WebM, Matroska and IVF carry it, decoded by Egami's Vp8StreamDecoder: every frame equals libvpx's sample for sample. Frames come out as 8-bit 4:2:0 planes, BT.601 limited range unless the container says otherwise; hidden frames (alternate references) give no frame. A WebM track with alpha (HasAlpha) decodes its second VP8 stream, carried in each packet's addition 1, into the frame's alpha plane unless DecodeAlpha is off. Encoding goes through CreateEncoder(VideoEncoderOptions, MediaContext).

Vp8EncoderOptions

The VP8 settings beyond VideoEncoderOptions, for experts: quantizer limits, alternate reference frames, golden frame spacing, token partitions and error resilience. Every one has a default that suits most uses.

Vp9Codec

VP9, as WebM, Matroska and IVF carry it, decoded in all four profiles: 8, 10 and 12 bit, 4:2:0, 4:2:2, 4:4:4 and RGB, with every frame equal to libvpx's sample for sample. Frames come out at their own coded size (a stream may change size between keyframes) as 8-bit planes, or 16-bit planes holding 10 or 12 bits; hidden frames (alternate references) give no frame. A WebM track with alpha (HasAlpha) decodes its second VP9 stream, carried in each packet's addition 1, into the frame's alpha plane unless DecodeAlpha is off. 4:4:0 streams are not decoded. Encoding goes through CreateEncoder(VideoEncoderOptions, MediaContext).

Vp9EncoderOptions

The VP9 settings beyond VideoEncoderOptions, for experts: quantizer limits, alternate reference frames, golden frame spacing, tile columns, adaptive quantization, error resilience, the bit depth and alpha. Every one has a default that suits most uses.

Interfaces

IVideoDecoder

Turns packets into frames with a send and receive loop. One packet can give no frame yet (a codec that holds frames back) or several, so after each Send(EncodedPacket) call TryReceive(out YuvVideoFrame?) until it returns false, and at the end of the stream call Flush() and receive what is left.

IVideoDecoder<TPixel>

A decoder that converts its frames to TPixel pixels. See IVideoDecoder.

IVideoEncoder

Turns frames into packets with a send and receive loop, the mirror of IVideoDecoder. After each Send(VideoFrame) call TryReceive(out EncodedPacket?) until it returns false; at the end call Flush() and receive what is left.
