How Trim(string, string, MediaTime, MediaTime?, TrimMode, Func<TrackInfo, bool>?, MediaContext?, IProgress<MediaProgress>?, CancellationToken) starts a cut that does not fall on a keyframe.

public enum TrimMode

Fields

Keyframe = 1

Copies every packet: the cut starts at the video keyframe at or before the start, so it may show a few frames more than asked, as ffmpeg -ss -c copy does. Nothing is decoded.

Reencode = 2

Decodes the video from the keyframe before the start and encodes every frame of the cut again, with the same codec at the quantizer the source had. Frame-exact like Smart, but slower and with a second generation of loss.

Smart = 0

The default: frame-exact, with the fewest frames encoded again. The frames from the start up to the next keyframe are decoded and encoded again as a new group that starts with a keyframe and matches the stream (codec, profile, size, bit depth, chroma format, color, and for AV1 the sequence header byte for byte), at the quantizer the frames it replaces had; every packet from that keyframe on is copied byte for byte. A start on a keyframe, and Motion JPEG or uncompressed video, are a plain copy. Codecs Egami.Video cannot encode (H.264, HEVC) start at the keyframe at or before the start, as Keyframe does; a stream the encoder cannot match is encoded again whole, as Reencode does. Both say so in a MediaWarning.

Examples

// Frame-exact: only the frames from 0.3 s up to the next keyframe are encoded again; every other packet is copied.
TrimResult result = MediaFile.Trim("show.webm", "cut.webm", MediaTime.FromSeconds(0.3), MediaTime.FromSeconds(0.8));
Console.WriteLine($"{result.Frames} frames from {result.Start.Seconds:0.000} s, {result.ReencodedFrames} encoded again");
foreach (MediaWarning warning in result.Warnings)
{
    Console.WriteLine(warning.Message);
}

// No decoding at all: the cut starts at the keyframe at or before 0.3 s.
MediaFile.Trim("show.webm", "copy.webm", MediaTime.FromSeconds(0.3), MediaTime.FromSeconds(0.8), TrimMode.Keyframe);
