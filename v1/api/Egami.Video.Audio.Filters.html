Namespace Egami.Video.Audio.Filters

Classes

AudioFade

Fades the sound in or out over a stretch of the stream's time line. A fade in is silent before Start and reaches full level on the exact sample at Start plus Duration; a fade out is full level before Start and silent from its end on. Times are on the frames' own timestamps, not counted from the first sample, and are rounded to whole samples; put a Retime first to count from zero. The curves give the same gains as ffmpeg's afade with the curve each value names. Works in place.

AudioFilter

The base of Egami.Video's own audio filters. It takes the rate and channels from the first frame, converts every frame to F32Planar once on the way in, and queues the output. Inside an AudioFilterChain the converted frames pass from filter to filter without another copy, and filters that keep the length (gain, fades, pan) work on them in place. To write a filter of your own, implement IAudioFilter.

AudioFilterChain

Runs several filters on one input in a single pass: each frame is converted to F32Planar once, then handed from filter to filter in order, in place where the filter keeps the length. The chain is itself an IAudioFilter, owns the filters added to it and disposes them with itself.

AudioMixer

Mixes several streams into one by adding them, each times its weight, like ffmpeg's amix with normalize=0. Inputs are placed on the output by their timestamps, so one that starts later comes in at its time; a gap inside an input is silence. Inputs at another rate or with other channels are resampled with Resampler and remixed with ChannelMixer's default matrix on the way in. The sum is not divided by the number of inputs unless Normalize is set, so a voice over music keeps its level; put a limiter after it if the sum can clip. Send each input's frames with Send(int, AudioFrame) and end each with Complete(int): output waits until every input has either sent something or ended.

Biquad

A second-order filter from the Audio EQ Cookbook: low and high pass, band pass, notch, all pass, peaking and shelves. The width is a Q (0.7071, the default, is the flattest low and high pass) or, with FromBandwidth(BiquadKind, double, double, double), a bandwidth in octaves. The filter runs in transposed direct form II with its state in double, so it stays quiet at low frequencies. For steeper low and high pass use Butterworth; for several bands in one filter, ParametricEqualizer.

Butterworth

A Butterworth low or high pass of order 2 to 8: maximally flat in the pass band, -3 dB at the corner and 6 dB more cut per octave for each order. It runs as a cascade of second-order sections (plus one first-order section for an odd order), each designed by the bilinear transform warped to the corner, so the corner is exact at any sample rate. Order 2 is the same as a Biquad with ButterworthQ.

Compressor

Turns the level down above a threshold, by Ratio, like ffmpeg's acompressor: a level 12 dB over the threshold comes out 3 dB over it at a ratio of 4. A soft Knee eases into the ratio, Attack and Release set how fast the gain moves, and Makeup turns the result back up. Every channel gets the same gain, from the loudest one.

Crossfade

Joins two streams with an overlap: the end of the first fades out while the start of the second fades in, like ffmpeg's acrossfade. The output is the first stream up to its last Duration, then the overlap mixed, then the rest of the second stream, so it lasts the two lengths minus the overlap. The overlap shortens to the shorter stream's length when one is shorter. Send each input's frames with Send(int, AudioFrame) and end each with Complete(int); frames of the second input wait until the first has ended. Both inputs must have the same rate and channels. Output starts at the first input's first timestamp and runs on without gaps.

Delay

Delays channels by putting silence in front of them, like ffmpeg's adelay: each channel can have its own delay, for example to line up microphones or fix lip sync. The output keeps the input's timestamps and gets longer by the longest delay; Flush() hands out the tail, so no input is lost.

Gain

Changes the level, by a fixed gain and, optionally, an Envelope that changes over time. A change to Decibels or Factor between frames moves to the new gain over 5 ms, so it does not click. Unity gain with no envelope passes samples through unchanged. Works in place; nothing is clipped, so a gain above 0 dB can take samples past full scale until an encoder or a limiter clamps them.

GainEnvelope

A gain that changes over time, in decibels, for Envelope: a list of Keyframes whose values are decibels. Before the first keyframe the gain is the first value, after the last one the last value, and between two the value eases from one to the next in decibels. A value of NegativeInfinity is silence; a segment to or from it moves in linear amplitude instead, so the fade still sounds smooth. Keyframe times are the frames' own timestamps, not counted from the first sample.

Gate

Turns the level down below a threshold, like ffmpeg's agate, to keep hiss and room noise out of the pauses: a level 1 dB under the threshold comes out Ratio dB under it, but never more than Range down. Attack sets how fast the gate opens when the level rises and Release how fast it closes. Every channel gets the same gain, from the loudest one.

Limiter

Keeps the level under a Ceiling, like ffmpeg's alimiter. It looks 5 ms ahead, so the gain is already down when a peak arrives and no sample goes over; the gain then comes back up over Release. With TruePeak on, it also limits the peaks between samples, found by 4x oversampling as ITU-R BS.1770 measures them, so a decoder or a sample rate converter downstream stays under the ceiling too. The output is held back by Latency and keeps the input's timestamps; Flush() hands out the rest.

Pad

Adds silence at the end of a stream, like ffmpeg's apad: a fixed amount, or as much as it takes for the stream to last at least a total length. The silence comes out on Flush(); the input passes through.

Pan

Places a mono or stereo input in a stereo output. Position pans a mono input with the constant-power law (-3 dB in each channel at the centre, like ffmpeg's pan=stereo|c0=0.7071c0|c1=0.7071c0); on a stereo input it turns one side down along the same law, scaled so the centre is unchanged. Balance turns one side down in a straight line. Swap exchanges left and right, and InvertLeft and InvertRight flip a channel's polarity. Changes to the position or balance between frames move over 5 ms. For any other channel matrix use ChannelMixer.

ParametricEqualizer

Several Biquad bands run as one filter, in the order given, like a mixing desk's EQ or a chain of ffmpeg's equalizer, bass and treble. Works in place.

Retime

Gives a stream new timestamps, like ffmpeg's asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS: the first sample gets Start and every later one follows on at the sample rate, so gaps and jitter in the input's timestamps go away.

Speed

Plays a stream faster or slower with the pitch following, like a tape or ffmpeg's asetrate followed by aresample: the samples are resampled with Resampler as if they had been recorded at the rate times Factor. The output keeps the sample rate and lasts the input's length divided by the factor; it starts at the input's first timestamp. Cheaper than Tempo, which keeps the pitch. The factor is used as a fraction with a denominator up to 1000.

Tempo

Changes the tempo and keeps the pitch, with WSOLA (waveform similarity overlap-add, after Verhelst and Roelands, 1993): the output is built of overlapping Hann windows of the input, each taken at the place near its ideal position whose waveform best continues the window before it. The output keeps the sample rate, starts at the input's first timestamp and lasts the input's length divided by Factor, rounded to the nearest sample. A factor of 1 passes samples through. For a speed change where the pitch follows, Speed is cheaper.

Trim

Keeps the part of a stream between two times on its time line, like ffmpeg's atrim, cut on the exact sample. The times are the frames' own timestamps, not counted from the first sample, so put a Retime first to count from zero. The samples kept keep their timestamps; follow with Retime to start the result at zero.

Structs

EqualizerBand

One band of a ParametricEqualizer: a Biquad shape, frequency, gain and Q.

Interfaces

IAudioFilter

A streaming audio filter: frames go in through Send(AudioFrame) and come out through TryReceive(out AudioFrame?), the same shape as Resampler, decoders and encoders, so filters, the resampler and encoders plug together. Frames of any sample format go in; frames come out as F32Planar with exact timestamps. Implement it to put a filter of your own in an AudioFilterChain.
