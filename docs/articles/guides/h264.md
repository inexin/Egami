# H.264

Egami.Video.H264 decodes H.264 (AVC), the codec of most MP4 and MOV files from phones, cameras and the web. It is a separate,
opt-in package because H.264 is covered by patents (see [Patents](#patents)); Egami.Video itself contains no H.264 decoder and
copies H.264 tracks through untouched when this package is not registered.

- Profiles: progressive Constrained Baseline, Main, High, High 10 and High 4:2:2, at 8 and 10 bits, 4:0:0, 4:2:0 and 4:2:2,
  and their Intra profiles. CAVLC and CABAC, B-frames with spatial and temporal direct prediction, weighted prediction, the
  8x8 transform and scaling matrices, long-term references and several slices per frame.
- Containers: MP4, MOV, Matroska and AVI through Egami.Video's readers, and raw Annex B `.h264` files through `H264Format`.
- Frames equal FFmpeg's decode sample for sample on every clip in the test corpus.
- Not decoded: interlaced streams (field and MBAFF pictures), 4:4:4 and lossless (High 4:4:4), and the Extended profile's
  data partitions, FMO, ASO and SP and SI slices. They give `UnsupportedFormatException`.

`WithH264()` (from `H264MediaContextExtensions`) adds the decoder, `H264Codec`, and the raw stream reader, `H264Format`, to a
`MediaContext`. Nothing registers them on its own; set the context as the default with `MediaContext.SetDefault` to use it

## Decoding

Frames come out in display order through `VideoReader`, like any other codec, as `YuvVideoFrame`s with the stream's own
planes (8-bit samples in bytes, 10-bit in ushorts) or converted to a pixel type. The colour comes from the stream's VUI, then
the container, and otherwise BT.601 for 576 lines and fewer and BT.709 above; HDR10 mastering display and content light level
SEI become the frame's `Hdr`.

[!code-csharp[](../../../tests/Egami.DocSamples/VideoH264.cs#decode)]

Broken data follows `VideoReaderOptions.Errors`. A slice that cannot be read reports `H264.CorruptSlice`, and the macroblocks
it leaves out are filled from the previous frame (`Conceal`, and `Skip`, the default) or the frame is finished and the error
thrown (`Throw`). Decoding that starts in the middle of a stream waits for the next IDR or recovery point; a reference frame
that is missing is replaced by the nearest one.

Slices decode in parallel when a frame has several, and deblocking runs on `EgamiContext.MaxParallelism` threads as a wave
down the frame.

## Raw .h264 files

`H264Format` reads raw Annex B streams (`.h264`, `.264` and `.avc`), as encoders and `ffmpeg -f h264` write them. Such a stream
has no timestamps, so frames are timed by the stream's VUI frame rate, or 25 frames a second, unless `FrameRate` is set.
Seeking goes back to the IDR frame before the target.

## Patents

H.264 is covered by patents. Egami's authors grant no patent rights and are not lawyers. Whether you need a patent licence
depends on your country and on how you use and distribute this package; commercial products may need one from the AVC patent
pools or holders. Egami.Video does not depend on this package.
