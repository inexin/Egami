# H.264

Egami.Video.H264 decodes and encodes H.264 (AVC), the codec of most MP4 and MOV files from phones, cameras and the web. It is a separate,
opt-in package because H.264 is covered by patents (see [Patents](#patents)); Egami.Video itself contains no H.264 decoder and
copies H.264 tracks through untouched when this package is not registered.

- Profiles: Constrained Baseline, Main, High, High 10 and High 4:2:2, at 8 and 10 bits, 4:0:0, 4:2:0 and 4:2:2, and their
  Intra profiles. CAVLC and CABAC, B-frames with spatial and temporal direct prediction, weighted prediction, the 8x8
  transform and scaling matrices, long-term references and several slices per frame.
- Interlaced streams: field pictures (PAFF) and MBAFF frames, whose macroblock pairs are each two frame or two field
  macroblocks. A frame's two fields come out woven into one frame, as FFmpeg gives them; see [Interlaced video](#interlaced-video).
- Containers: MP4, MOV, Matroska and AVI through Egami.Video's readers, and raw Annex B `.h264` files through `H264Format`.
- Frames equal FFmpeg's decode sample for sample on every clip in the test corpus and on the interlaced and FRExt
  conformance bitstreams of ITU-T H.264.1.
- Not decoded: 4:4:4 and lossless (High 4:4:4), and the Extended profile's data partitions, FMO, ASO and SP and SI slices.
  They give `UnsupportedFormatException`.

## Decoding

Frames come out in display order through `VideoReader`, like any other codec, as `YuvVideoFrame`s with the stream's own
planes (8-bit samples in bytes, 10-bit in ushorts) or converted to a pixel type. The colour comes from the stream's VUI, then
the container, and otherwise BT.601 for 576 lines and fewer and BT.709 above; HDR10 mastering display and content light level
SEI become the frame's `Hdr`, and HDR10+ user data SEI its `Hdr10Plus`.

[!code-csharp[](../../../tests/Egami.DocSamples/VideoH264.cs#decode)]

Broken data follows `VideoReaderOptions.Errors`. A slice that cannot be read reports `H264.CorruptFrame`, and the macroblocks
it leaves out are filled from the previous frame (`Conceal`, and `Skip`, the default) or the frame is finished and the error
thrown (`Throw`). Decoding that starts in the middle of a stream waits for the next IDR or recovery point; a reference frame
that is missing is replaced by the nearest one.

Slices decode in parallel when a frame has several, and deblocking runs on `EgamiContext.MaxParallelism` threads as a wave
down the frame.

## Interlaced video

An interlaced frame holds two fields, every other row each, filmed one after the other. The decoder gives the frame with both
fields woven together and says how on the frame: `FieldOrder` is `TopFieldFirst` or `BottomFieldFirst` for an interlaced
frame and `Progressive` otherwise, and `RepeatFields` counts the fields a frame is shown for beyond its two (3:2 pulldown and
frame doubling). Both come from the picture timing SEI's `pic_struct` when the stream has it, else from how the frame was coded
(two field pictures or an MBAFF frame) and the order of its fields, as ffprobe reports them. Deinterlacing is up to you;
`InterlaceDetector` finds interlaced stretches in decoded video.

[!code-csharp[](../../../tests/Egami.DocSamples/VideoH264.cs#fields)]

A field without its second field, as a cut stream can end, comes out with its rows doubled into the missing field and reports
`H264.UnpairedField`.

## Encoding

`H264Codec.Instance` encodes through `VideoWriter` (or `CreateEncoder` for packets): 8-bit 4:2:0 progressive frames, with an
even width and height. `Quality` (0 to 100, default 60; 50 is about x264's default CRF 23) and `Effort` (0 to 10, default 4)
work as for Egami's other encoders. `H264EncoderOptions` holds the H.264 settings: the `H264Profile` (High by default,
Main, or Constrained Baseline for the simplest players), the level (the lowest that the frame size and rate fit, unless set),
the `H264EntropyCoder` (CABAC, or CAVLC), slices, QP limits, adaptive quantization and Annex B packets for raw streams. HDR metadata on
the first frame goes into the stream as mastering display and content light level SEI. Every picture the encoder makes decodes
in Egami's decoder, and FFmpeg's, to the encoder's own reconstruction, sample for sample.

Keyframes are IDR pictures. P pictures are predicted from the frames before: one reference at effort 0 and 1, two at 2 and 3,
three from 4, four from 9 (fewer when the level's buffer is smaller). Fades are predicted with explicit weights, except in
Constrained Baseline, which has none. Between them come B pictures, predicted from the frames on both sides and coded with fewer bits:
`H264EncoderOptions.BFrames` caps a run (3 by default, none at effort 0), and the encoder picks each run's length from the
content. From effort 4 a run of two or three is a pyramid, whose middle B picture the others predict from; from effort 8 each B
picture tries spatial and temporal direct prediction and keeps the smaller. B-frames need `LatencyMode.Quality` (the default):
they are coded out of order, so packets carry decode timestamps. Constrained Baseline has none. `AdaptiveQuantization` (on by default) codes busy macroblocks coarser and flat ones
finer, which looks better at the same size but lowers PSNR: turn it off to measure PSNR.

`RateControlMode.Bitrate` (one pass, or two with `FirstPassStats`) and `RateControlMode.ConstantBitrate` aim at `Bitrate`; the
level chosen then fits the peak bitrate and buffer too. Each picture is cut into `H264EncoderOptions.Slices` bands of macroblock
rows (by default one per 1800 macroblocks, up to eight), coded in parallel on `EgamiContext.MaxParallelism` threads; nothing is
predicted across a slice edge, which costs a little compression, and the stream is the same for any number of threads.

### Compression and speed

The encoder is measured against x264 0.164 on four Xiph test clips (crowd_run, ducks_take_off, park_joy and old_town_cross at
720p, 50 frames a second), both tuned for PSNR. At effort 4 it needs 5 to 18 % more bits than x264's medium preset for the same
PSNR (11.6 % on average), about what x264's faster preset gives, and it is slower: on one thread, with x264 at the CRF that
gives the same PSNR-Y, 4.3 times x264 medium's CPU cycles a frame at 720p at quality 50 (3.4 to 6.4 by clip) and 4.4 times at
quality 34 (3.7 to 5.8), on .NET's default runtime with tiered PGO on and without adaptive quantization; with it, the
default, 4.6 and 4.7 times. The design's goal of twice x264 medium's time is not met. FFmpeg and the JM reference decoder decode every stream the encoder writes to the same frames as
Egami's own decoder.

#### Speed by effort

Each effort costs more cycles than the one below it and needs fewer bits, on the mean of the four clips. Cycles are a
macroblock (a 720p frame has 3600) at one thread, quality 50, the mean of the clips; x264 medium is timed at the bitrate effort
4 gives. BD-rate is PSNR-Y against x264 medium over four qualities and 60 frames: the mean of the clips and the worst clip.

| 1 | 139k | 2.1 (1.7–2.9) | +27.4 % | +41.2 % |
| 2 | 175k | 2.6 (2.2–3.5) | +22.3 % | +30.7 % |
| 3 | 251k | 3.7 (3.2–4.7) | +12.3 % | +17.9 % |
| 4 (default) | 263k | 4.0 (3.5–5.2) | +11.6 % | +17.5 % |
| 5 | 288k | 4.3 (3.9–5.3) | +11.5 % | +17.5 % |
| 6 | 313k | 4.7 (4.0–6.2) | +10.8 % | +17.0 % |
| 7 | 393k | 5.9 (5.1–7.6) | +10.6 % | +16.8 % |
| 8 | 489k | 7.4 (6.2–10.0) | +10.5 % | +16.7 % |

What each effort adds:

- **0**: one reference, no B pictures, 16x16 partitions only, a diamond search, intra only where inter is poor.
- **1**: B pictures, 16x8 and 8x16 partitions, Intra8x8.
- **2**: two references, 8x8 partitions, a hexagon search and the 8x8 transform for inter blocks.
- **3**: RDOQ and the second-best partitioning RD-coded when it comes close.
- **4** (default): B pyramids, three references, the full sub-sample search in P pictures; B pictures search one list 0
  reference and try intra only where its 16x16 prediction comes below the inter cost by SATD. A 16x16 candidate whose search
  cost is low for the QP is coded at once, and when it leaves no residual nothing else but intra is searched or coded.
- **5**: two list 0 references in B pictures, intra in B pictures where it comes within 1.5 times the inter cost (as in P
  pictures), and one more sub-sample refinement round.
- **6**: 16x8 and 8x16 searched in every macroblock (not only where 8x8 beats 16x16); a wider search range; 8x4, 4x8 and 4x4 at
  levels up to 3.
- **7**: intra tried in every inter macroblock.
- **8**: the full sub-sample search in B pictures too, temporal direct where it codes smaller, and the second-best partitioning
  always RD-coded.
- **9**: four references.
- **10**: every intra kind (16x16, 4x4 and 8x8), B_Direct_16x16 and both inter transforms always RD-coded.

tools/video/time-h264-encoder.py and check-h264-encoder.py in the repository repeat these measurements, and
profile-h264-encoder.py shows where the time goes (`baseline` writes this table).

## Raw .h264 files

`H264Format` reads raw Annex B streams (`.h264`, `.264` and `.avc`), as encoders and `ffmpeg -f h264` write them. Such a stream
has no timestamps, so frames are timed by the stream's VUI frame rate, or 25 frames a second, unless `FrameRate` is set.
Seeking goes back to the IDR frame before the target.
